THE SHORT VERSION

Interoperability is the recurring constraint

Across projects, users repeatedly ask agents to work with more providers, tools and systems. The opportunity is rarely “another chatbot.” It is reducing the work required to connect, switch, govern and recover an agent workflow in a real environment.

GitHub Issues are evidence of friction, not proof that someone will pay. Treat every signal below as an interview lead: find the affected role, learn what they tried, and ask what the delay or workaround costs.

HOW TO USE THIS REPORT

One week, one assumption

RECURRENT, CROSS-PROJECT FRICTION

Three signals to test before chasing a trend

01 · 8 SIGNALS / 8 PROJECTS

Provider interoperability

Builders encounter friction when models, providers and tools must work together. The visible demand is for reliable switching, compatibility and connection workflows—not simply more model choices.

Interview question: “Where does a provider or tool change create manual rework, broken context, or an approval delay in your agent workflow?”

02 · 8 SIGNALS / 5 PROJECTS

Tool & system connectors

Teams want agents to operate across existing systems, but integration depth, permissions and maintenance are persistent obstacles. A narrow connector for one costly workflow can be more valuable than a generic agent platform.

Interview question: “Which system should your agent update or retrieve from, and what makes you avoid automating it today?”

Read source-backed signals
03 · 8 SIGNALS / 8 PROJECTS

Product capability workflows

Requests often concern missing end-to-end actions rather than a missing model. Look for a repeatable before-and-after workflow: a task a user cannot finish, must stitch together manually, or cannot verify safely.

Interview question: “What outcome do you still complete outside the agent, and what would have to be true for you to trust it end to end?”

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

What this report does—and does not—claim

These signals come from public, qualified GitHub Issues attached to active open-source projects. They show recurring technical and workflow friction. They do not prove market size, urgency, budget or willingness to pay.

The next step is not building. It is a short, structured conversation with people who own the workflow.
